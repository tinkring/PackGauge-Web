At the workbench

Connect. Scan.
Take a closer look.

No laptop needed for scanning. A straightforward workflow, from the first connection to the next saved record.

  1. 01 / CONNECT

    Power up.

    Provide USB-C power. Inspect the pack and connect a supported battery using the documented interface.

  2. 02 / SCAN

    Tap Scan Pack.

    Let the reader request the available data, then move through Pack, Charge, Tool, Amps, and Conditions.

  3. 03 / REVIEW

    Keep the details.

    Review the readings, add a note, revisit saved scans, compare records, or send a configured email log.

How PackGauge started

It started with a simple question
from my dad.

My dad noticed that the battery packs for his cordless mower would indicate a full charge while on the charger, but after removing them and pressing the battery’s fuel-gauge button, they would sometimes show only three bars instead of four. He asked me what might cause that.

I started researching how the packs worked and learned more about the individual cell groups inside them and how differences in their voltages can affect the overall health and performance of a battery pack. The problem was that checking those voltages normally meant opening the pack and measuring each group individually — something I wanted to avoid.

While researching, I came across an open-source battery communication project by mnh-jansson and its contributors. I was amazed by how much information they had been able to decode directly from the battery’s communication interface. Suddenly, it looked possible to see the individual cell-group voltages and other useful battery data without opening the pack at all.

That immediately led to another thought: why should I need a laptop every time I want to check a battery?

I started looking for a small standalone platform and found the inexpensive ESP32-based display commonly known as the Cheap Yellow Display. It already combined a touchscreen, an ESP32 microcontroller, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth in one compact package. It was more than capable of what I originally had in mind — and opened the door to doing quite a bit more.

The idea grew from simply displaying cell voltages into a handheld battery diagnostic tool that can show cell imbalance, charging and tool-use history, fault counters, current-load and condition charts, save scans, and send results over Wi-Fi.

With the hardware selected and the battery communication work provided by the open-source community as a foundation, I began developing PackGauge with the help of ChatGPT and Claude.

What started as a simple question about one of my dad’s batteries became PackGauge — a standalone tool built to make battery health easier to see, understand, and share, without opening the pack or connecting it to a computer.

Before you connect

Know what
you're looking at.

Which batteries is it designed for?

18V Red Tool Packs. You know the ones. PackGauge is designed for compatible packs in that ecosystem—not every battery platform. Pack hardware and firmware revisions affect the available data. Read the compatibility details.

Are these the real screens?

Yes—the public artwork is rebuilt from actual current prototype screenshots. The layout, controls, and data categories are real; identifying serial information and third-party-specific labels are sanitized in the website renders.

Does an on-screen status mean a battery is safe?

No. Interface labels and colored readings are diagnostic cues, not an independent health, capacity, authenticity, or safety certification. A small voltage spread or missing fault counter is not a pass result.

Does it repair or reset a battery?

No. The current user workflow reads diagnostic data; it does not offer repair, reset, rebalance, unlock, or reprogram controls. Scanning may cause a pack to refresh its own recorded statistics.

Do I need a computer or Wi-Fi to scan?

The standalone reader is USB-powered and does not need a computer for scanning. Wi-Fi is used for configured network features such as sending email. Saved scan records use micro-SD.
