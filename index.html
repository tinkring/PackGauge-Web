01 / Diagnostics

One scan. The pack’s own story.

PackGauge reads what the battery management system records and keeps unknown or unavailable data honest—never turning an empty register into a reassuring zero.

01

Cell-bank visibility

Find the spread a pack-voltage reading hides.

See all five series cell banks individually, identify the high and low bank, and read the total spread in millivolts. PackGauge labels imbalance as Normal, Minor, Significant, or Severe without pretending voltage alone is a capacity test.

02

Charging history

Review total, Redlink, and dumb charge counts; charge and idle time; low-voltage charge starts; and charge-condition distributions when recorded by the pack.

03

Tool-use history

Inspect total discharged amp-hours, estimated equivalent cycles for known pack types, time under load, and 5A or 10A discharge-current histograms.

04

Fault evidence

Surface recorded discharged-to-empty, overheat, overcurrent, low-voltage, and low-voltage bounce counters where that firmware provides them.

05

Evidence you can keep

Write every scan to micro-SD as a full CSV and spreadsheet-ready column, add a note, browse saved scans, compare readings, or email the files.

02 / How it works

Connect. Scan. Understand.

  1. 01

    Connect the pack

    Power PackGauge over USB-C, then connect a tested M18 pack through the purpose-built signal interface. The battery remains a data source, not a power supply.

  2. 02

    Read the registers

    Tap Scan Pack. PackGauge issues the wake and read sequence that causes the pack to refresh its reported statistics, then walks the full 184-register address map in roughly 5–10 seconds. It reports unavailable blocks as unavailable.

  3. 03

    Inspect and archive

    Move through Pack, Chg, Tool, Amps, and Cond views. The scan is stored on micro-SD for later review and comparison.

Read-only user workflow.

The current product workflow requests diagnostic data and does not offer controls to change pack configuration, erase history, or set stored values. The pack may update its own reported statistics during the scan sequence.

Tested type codes are not a guarantee.

Pack firmware and hardware revisions can differ. An unlisted type may still expose core identity and cell data, while a listed type may expose fewer fields. Capacity-based cycle estimates require a recognized type and remain estimates.

Forge note Forge firmware tested by the project exposes identity, cells, dates, and core charge/discharge totals—but not the fault, load-histogram, or charge-condition blocks found on many earlier packs. PackGauge reports those blocks as unavailable instead of zero.

05 / Safety & limitations

A scan is evidence—not a safety certificate.

PackGauge reports electrical readings and history made available by the connected pack. Those observations are useful diagnostic inputs, but they cannot establish that a lithium-ion battery is safe, undamaged, authentic, within warranty, or fit for continued use.

Observed

What PackGauge can show

Available cell-bank voltage, temperature, identity fields, counters, and history records at the time of the scan, plus clearly identified calculations such as voltage spread and estimated equivalent cycles.

Not established

What a scan cannot prove

Balanced voltage, a “Normal” band, or no recorded faults does not prove capacity, internal condition, electrical integrity, future performance, or safety. Missing data is not a reassuring zero.

Stop and inspect

Visible damage overrides data

Do not rely on PackGauge to clear a pack that is damaged, swollen, leaking, unusually hot, wet, modified, recalled, or otherwise suspect. Stop using it and follow the battery manufacturer’s safety and service guidance.

Diagnostic only.

PackGauge does not charge, repair, rebalance, reset, authenticate, or certify a battery pack, and it does not replace manufacturer instructions or appropriate electrical and physical inspection. The current user workflow does not expose pack-write commands.

09 / About PackGauge

A diagnostic instrument built from measured behavior.

PackGauge grew through direct testing against real packs and publicly documented protocol research. The project documents where pack generations differ, validates its decoder against reference output, and treats missing data as a fact—not a number to fill in.
