Meet the workflow ↓

From pack to perspective

Connect. Scan.
Take a closer look.

No laptop on the workbench. PackGauge brings the available readings and history together on the reader, with records you can revisit later.

  1. 01 / CONNECT

    Power up. Connect.

    Provide USB-C power and connect a compatible pack through the documented signal interface. Inspect the pack and follow the safety guidance before connecting.

  2. 02 / SCAN

    Let the reader ask.

    Start a scan from the touchscreen. PackGauge requests the available pack data and organizes it into readings, records, and charts.

  3. 03 / EXPLORE

    Keep the useful details.

    Move through the result pages, save scans to micro-SD, and return to the readings when you need to compare or share them.

Do not connect damaged, swollen, wet, unusually hot, or otherwise suspect packs. Read safety & diagnostic limits →

Good questions

Before you
connect a pack.

Which batteries is it designed for?

Compatible Milwaukee M18 battery packs—not every battery platform. Hardware and firmware revisions affect what can be read. Read the compatibility details.

Does it tell me whether a battery is healthy?

It displays available pack-reported data, not an independent health, capacity, authenticity, or safety certification. A small voltage spread or an absent fault counter is not a pass result.

Does it repair or reset a battery?

No. The current user workflow reads diagnostic data; it does not offer repair, reset, rebalance, unlock, or reprogram controls. A scan can cause the pack to refresh its own recorded statistics.

Do I need a computer or Wi-Fi to scan?

The standalone reader is USB-powered and scans without a computer. Wi-Fi is used for network features such as emailing logs after configuration; scan storage uses micro-SD.
